Agentic AI Portfolio Project · Interview-style problem statement

ClaimSense: Build an Agentic Motor Insurance Claims Processor

Read claim documents, decide coverage by citing the policy wording, calculate the exact payable amount, catch suspicious claims, and keep a human in charge of every rejection, then deploy it on AWS.

Author: Shivank Agrawal Level: Advanced Effort: ~9 weeks part-time (10 hrs/week) Roles: FDE · AI Engineer · Applied ML

1The scenario

You have just joined NorthStar General Insurance (a fictional Indian insurer) as a Forward Deployed Engineer. On day one, the Head of Motor Claims tells you:

“We settle about 1,200 private-car claims a month. Every claim is read by a person: the claim form, the surveyor's report, sometimes a police FIR. Small, obvious claims wait in the same queue as complex ones, so customers wait a week for a ₹12,000 bumper repair. Meanwhile some fraudulent claims slip through because nobody has time to compare documents carefully. I want an AI system that clears the easy claims in minutes, sends the risky ones to the right expert with everything they need, and never rejects a customer without a human signing off. And I need to see the cost per claim and an audit trail for the regulator.”

Your job is to design, build, evaluate and deploy that system. The numbers above are part of the scenario, not real company data.

2Motor insurance in 5 minutes

You don't need insurance experience. These are the only terms you need:

TermMeaningWhy it matters for the code
Comprehensive (Package) policyCovers damage to your own car (Section I) and liability to others (Section II)Only these policies can pay for own damage
Third Party (Liability Only)Covers only damage you cause to othersOwn-damage claim on a TP policy must be rejected
IDV (Insured Declared Value)Roughly the car's market value. The maximum the insurer paysCap every payout at IDV
DepreciationReplaced parts are paid at a discount (plastic 50%, metal by car age…)Must be calculated line by line from the surveyor's table
Zero Depreciation add-onCustomer paid extra so no depreciation is deductedChanges the payable amount a lot
Compulsory deductibleFixed amount the customer always bears (₹1,000 or ₹2,000)Subtract from every claim
SurveyorIndependent licensed inspector who examines the car and assesses the lossThe surveyor's report is the source of truth for amounts
FIRPolice First Information ReportMay reveal drunk driving or a different accident date
Total loss / CTLRepair cost above 75% of IDV, so the car is a write-offSettled on IDV, always needs a claims officer
SIUSpecial Investigation Unit: the insurer's fraud teamSuspicious claims go here instead of to a normal adjuster

3What you are given

📄 data/policy_wording.md19 numbered clauses (OD-1, DEP-1, EXC-4, LIM-1…) of a private car package policy.
🗄️ data/claims.db (SQLite)policies (25), prior_claims, garages (13, two on a fraud watchlist).
📁 data/sample_claims/20 claims (CLM-1001…1020). Each has claim_form.pdf, survey_report.pdf and sometimes fir.pdf.
✅ data/eval_labels.csvThe correct route, recommendation, fraud label and payable amount for every sample claim.

The PDFs use Indian formats on purpose: dates like 21/10/2026, times like 6:40 PM, amounts like Rs. 22,500. Some claims contain traps: a drunk-driving note only in the FIR, a story that contradicts the surveyor, an accident date that doesn't match the police record.

Regenerate everything at any time with python -m scripts.generate_data (no API key needed).

4What you must build

A system that takes the PDFs of one claim and produces one of three outcomes:

RouteWhenWhat happens
AUTO_APPROVECovered, low fraud risk, amount within limit, confident decisionClaim is approved and the customer letter is sent. No human involved.
ADJUSTER_REVIEWLarge amount, total loss, medium fraud score, uncertain coverage, or any recommended rejectionClaim waits in the adjuster queue with the AI's recommendation and evidence.
SIU_INVESTIGATIONHigh fraud scoreClaim waits in the fraud team's queue with every red flag listed.

For every claim, the system must also store: extracted document data, policy check result, coverage decision with clause citations, the full payable calculation, fraud signals, the route, an audit trail of every step, the cost of the LLM calls, and finally the customer letter.

5Business rules (from the Head of Claims)

RuleValue
Auto-approve limitPayable ≤ ₹50,000
Fraud score for adjuster review25–49
Fraud score for SIU≥ 50
Total lossSurveyor's assessed cost > 75% of IDV → pay IDV minus deductible, human approval
Late reportingMore than 7 days after the accident is a fraud signal, not a rejection
New policyAccident within 30 days of policy start is a fraud signal
Inflated estimateGarage estimate > 140% of surveyor's assessment is a fraud signal
Repeat claimant2+ claims on the policy in the previous 12 months is a fraud signal
RejectionsThe AI may recommend a rejection but must never reject on its own

You decide how many points each fraud signal is worth, but you must be able to explain the choice.

6Non-functional requirements

  • Explainable money: every rupee of the payable amount must be traceable to a line in the surveyor's table and a clause in the policy.
  • Grounded coverage decisions: an exclusion must cite a clause ID that actually exists in the policy wording.
  • Pause and resume: a claim waiting for a human must survive a server restart.
  • Audit trail: who (agent or which person) did what, when, with what result.
  • Cost: report the LLM cost per claim. Target: under ₹2 per claim.
  • Latency: under 30 seconds per claim end to end.
  • Secrets: the OpenAI key must never be in code, Docker images or Terraform state.
  • Integration: a REST API so the insurer's other systems can submit claims and fetch decisions.

7Milestones and acceptance criteria

~1 week

M1 · Domain and data

Load the policies, garages and prior claims. Write a function that returns a policy by number and counts prior claims in the last 12 months.

✔ Done when: you can explain every table and every column, and query CLM-1018's policy history.

~1.5 weeks

M2 · Document intake

Turn each PDF into typed JSON with an LLM: claim form, survey report (including the line-item table), FIR. Normalise dates to YYYY-MM-DD, times to 24-hour, amounts to numbers.

✔ Done when: all 20 claims extract without errors and the survey line items add up to the printed total.

~2 weeks

M3 · The agent (LangGraph)

Build the steps: policy check → coverage check (retrieve clauses, LLM decides, cite clause IDs) → amount assessment (depreciation, deductible, IDV cap, total loss) → fraud check (rules + document contradictions) → routing. Skip steps that are pointless when the policy check already failed.

✔ Done when: CLM-1001 is auto-approved for exactly ₹12,050, CLM-1014 is recommended for rejection citing the drunk-driving clause, and CLM-1019 goes to SIU.

~1.5 weeks

M4 · Human-in-the-loop and portals

Pause the graph for adjuster / SIU decisions and resume it later. Build a REST API and a simple portal with four views: claimant, adjuster, SIU, dashboard. Overriding the AI must require a written note.

✔ Done when: you can stop the server, restart it, approve a waiting claim, and the customer letter is generated.

~1.5 weeks

M5 · Evals and observability

Run all 20 labelled claims and report route accuracy, recommendation accuracy, fraud precision and recall, payable accuracy, straight-through rate, cost and latency per claim.

✔ Done when: one command prints the scorecard, and you can explain every wrong answer.

~1.5 weeks

M6 · Deployment

Containerise the API and portal. Deploy to AWS with infrastructure as code. The OpenAI key must come from a secrets store. Provide a one-command teardown.

✔ Done when: a reviewer opens your public URL, processes a sample claim, and you then delete every resource with one command.

8How your work will be judged

AreaWeightWhat a strong submission shows
Correctness30%Eval scorecard, exact payable amounts, no claim lost in a FAILED state without a reason
Architecture choices25%Clear split: what the LLM does vs what code does, and why
Safety and auditability20%Grounded citations, human sign-off on rejections, audit trail, secrets handling
Production readiness15%API, pause/resume, Docker, IaC deployment and teardown, cost tracking
Communication10%README, architecture diagram, can defend trade-offs in an interview
Interview reality check: you will be asked “Why didn't you let the LLM calculate the amount?”, “What happens if the model invents a clause?”, “How do you know your fraud check works?”, and “What does one claim cost?” Build so that you can answer each with a number or a line of code.

9Hints (read only if stuck)

  • Use OpenAI structured outputs with Pydantic models so extraction always returns valid JSON.
  • Modern OpenAI models can read a PDF passed directly in the request, so you may not need an OCR library.
  • Extract each document in a separate call. Ask yourself why that helps fraud detection.
  • Chunk the policy wording by clause, not by a fixed number of tokens.
  • Look at LangGraph's interrupt() and checkpointers for pause/resume.
  • Money and thresholds belong in plain Python. LLMs read and judge; code calculates.
  • Your eval labels contain the correct payable amount. A wrong amount almost always means the survey table was read wrongly.

10What goes on your CV

Built ClaimSense, a multi-agent motor insurance claims system (LangGraph, OpenAI, FastAPI): reads claim PDFs, decides coverage with clause-cited RAG and routes claims to auto-approval, adjusters or a fraud unit with human-in-the-loop. Achieved X% routing accuracy and Y% fraud recall on a labelled test set at ₹Z per claim; deployed on AWS ECS Fargate with Terraform.

Replace X, Y, Z with your own eval numbers. Never put a number on your CV that you can't reproduce live.

Stretch goals (pick one to stand out)

  • Accept damage photos and let the model compare them with the surveyor's damaged areas.
  • Add a WhatsApp or email intake channel.
  • Swap SQLite for PostgreSQL (Amazon RDS) and use LangGraph's Postgres checkpointer.
  • Add tracing (LangSmith or Langfuse) and a dashboard of cost and latency per step.
  • Add 30 more labelled claims, including tricky ones you design yourself.
